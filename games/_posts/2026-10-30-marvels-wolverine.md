I've really enjoyed every Insomniac game that I've played, so I was very excited for Wolverine.  I was a little worried when initial reviews were mixed, but it delivered a perfectly sized, fun adventure game.

As opposed to the open-world sandbox of the Spider-Man games, Marvel's Wolverine is a linear adventure that can be completed in under 20 hours, or a bit more if you're cleaning up all the collectibles and achievements.  While I do enjoy an open-world game every now and again, and I loved the Spider-Man games, I also really appreciate having smaller experiences like this.  There's no filler content here.  Wolverine tells the story it wants to tell and wraps up.

Almost all of my Marvel knowledge is from the movies.  I'm no Wolverine expert, but I thought the story here was good and kept me wanting to see what would happen next.  The only moment that didn't feel quite right to me was how quickly Logan turned on the character who turned out to be the main villain.  There could have been a bit more time spent on this reveal and Logan coming to terms with it.

The true star of the story is the relationship between Logan and Jean.  It is well told and well acted.  The animation work on these characters is some of the best I can remember, and that was so important for the emotional beats to land.  The facial animations in particular really helped sell some of the harder-hitting moments.

The graphics in Marvel's Wolverine are stunning, but the character model for Wolverine himself bothered me at first.  I felt like it was trying to be Hugh Jackman without being Hugh Jackman.  By the time I had finished, however, I ended up really liking it.

The combat in Wolverine was quite fun.  Sure, it can be a bit button mashy, and the enemy variety is a little lacking, but I still found that it has a very satisfying rhythm to it.  When I was in sync with an encounter, using my different skills and parries, combat fell into a flow state and felt great.  Conversely, when I was trying to force the use of one skill or another, that flow never materialized, and I was much more likely to fail.

There are enough collectibles close enough to the main path that you'll be able to upgrade your health without having to resort to a guide, but there are still some hidden away in trickier-to-find spots.  Even better, Wolverine tells you exactly how many collectibles are in each chapter, and lets you quickly pop back to mop up any you missed.

Overall, I had a good time playing Marvel's Wolverine.  It does what it sets out to do: to tell a story about Logan and Jean, wrapped in a fun adventure.
