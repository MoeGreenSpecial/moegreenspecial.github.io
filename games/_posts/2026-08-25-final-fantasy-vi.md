Having finished and enjoyed [Chrono Trigger]({% post_url games/2026-04-02-chrono-trigger %}) earlier this year, I thought it would be fun to also play Final Fantasy VI, as it's the other major JRPG of the era and shares some of the same creative team at Square.  I wound up enjoying myself, but liking it just a bit less than Chrono Trigger which felt very focused and well paced compared to the much more sprawling scope of Final Fantasy VI.

Final Fantasy VI has a very large cast of characters, which is both a benefit and a detriment to the game.  On the one hand, having such a large cast, and having each character not always be available, means that the game doesn't feel like the journey of a single hero but rather that of the combined struggles and successes of the whole group.  On the other hand, having such a large cast meant that it was hard to get to know each of the characters well and develop a connection to them.  In the end, I focused primarily on Terra and Celes, and didn't develop much of a relationship with many of the other character's stories.

Structurally, Final Fantasy VI starts out fairly linear, but it hits an incredibly unique moment at the mid-point.  Instead of the party being able to overcome Kefka, they fail, he destroys the world, and you must regroup for the second half which is significantly more open.  That structure, especially in a game from the early 1990s, was surprising and is probably the single thing I am most going to remember the game for.  That being said, the shift to the more open format did not help with the sprawl of the game and the pacing slowed down significantly in its second half.  I often ran into issues where I didn't know what to do next; I think the game just wanted you to roam around to find another cast member to bring back to the party, and that didn't feel great from a pacing perspective.

The dungeons in this game are also a highlight, with each one being unique and none of them feeling tedious or repetitive.  The Opera in particular is a standout moment in the game, featuring a full musical sequence with Celes singing an aria to buy time for the rest of the party to fight off the backstage enemies. It's a dungeon where your main task is correctly performing a fake opera rather than focusing entirely on battles, and it's one of the best scenes in the game.

And the music should be mentioned specifically, since aside from the structure of the game, the music is the thing that most stood out to me.  Not only is the music fantastic, but it does a great job of providing identity to each of the major areas in the game.  The Zozo theme in particular really stood out to me.  The pixel remaster contains updated music, and it fit in very well even with the older art style.

Final Fantasy VI is also very much a 1990s JRPG, and suffers from some quality of life issues that wouldn't be present in a more modern game design.  There are the typical random encounters that occur as you're walking around, which generally aren't so bad but do get really annoying when you're walking around a dungeon trying to figure out a puzzle.  In several dungeons, I was wandering around the space to try to solve how to advance, and having random encounters pop up during that got frustrating quickly.  They caused me to not want to explore to solve the problem, which is exactly the opposite of what you want when presenting a puzzle to the player.  Thankfully the pixel remaster lets you disable the random encounters, which made doing these puzzle sections much more fun.  While I never felt like I had to grind out random encounters to keep my level up, having them at all stood in stark contrast to Chrono Trigger's approach of showing you the enemies right on the map.  Chrono Trigger's battles also occurring on the map made them feel more a part of the game than the Final Fantasy VI approach of whisking you away to a battle screen and then back again.

I also found that there were several key pieces of information that the game does not convey to the player. It never explains what your abilities and spells do, nor what each of the status ailments are or how to fix them. 

When I finished, I was glad that I had experienced Final Fantasy VI, even if it never quite matched the focused experience that Chrono Trigger delivered. The decision to have Kefka actually _win_ in this game is bold, and something I'll always remember it for, but also led to a much less focused experience in the second half.
