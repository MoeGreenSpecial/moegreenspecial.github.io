Playing Halo: Campaign Evolved brought back strong memories of what I enjoyed so much about Halo: Combat Evolved while also providing an impressive graphical overhaul. The original is a hall of fame game, and as a faithful remake, Halo: Campaign Evolved is also an incredible FPS experience.  When I say faithful, I mean it.  This is Halo: Combat Evolved remade, there are only a few small changes to the original game outside the modernization of the presentation.  You're not getting a new Halo experience here, just a beautiful version of the original game.  It is beautiful though; the graphical updates here are top-notch, and more importantly Halo Studios has really captured the feel of Halo through their port to the Unreal Engine.  This game feels great to play, looks and sounds incredible, and is well worth the 10-hour investment.  

This is Halo: Combat Evolved carefully rebuilt in Unreal, to the point that I clearly remember several of the individual rooms and encounters.  Especially the Warthog sequence to end the game, which remains one of the series highlights. The cut scenes are also extremely good, and notably do a fantastic job of highlighting how large the Covenant are compared to humans.  Needlers always felt like small weapons to me, but in one scene you see a human pick one up and it's gigantic.

This is the first version of Halo that I've played where the Flood really felt like a zombie menace.  It was always implied in previous Halo games, but the graphical updates here really help drive that point home.  I don't recall the Infectors being able to re-animate the Human and Sangheili forms before.  And this led to a cycle of killing Carriers which launched more Infectors, which re-infected Flood that were already dead, leading to the Flood levels being even more challenging than I remember.

Of course, infamously, The Library remains.  And that is still my least favorite of any Halo level.

Unfortunately, going from the iconic ending sequence of Halo straight into the bonus levels was a bit of a letdown.  The bonus levels are fine, but they feel like a bit of experimentation from Halo Studios.  The first of the three, in particular, is just a sequence of waves of enemies coming at you and doesn't seem to justify being there. It's a major drop from where you left off if you played it right after the end of the original game's campaign.  Although I appreciated what they were trying to do by the end of the third bonus level, none of them really shined.  This was the place that Halo Studios could have added something new to Halo, and it didn't manage to live up to the heights of the game that they remade.

But in the end, this is a graphically impressive way to replay one of my favorite games of all time.  Even though The Library was still involved.
