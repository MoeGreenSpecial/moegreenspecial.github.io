007 First Light is a great James Bond adventure, but I wish it were a little more Hitman and a little less standard action fare.  While it may not be reasonable to put the Hitman expectations on a game just because IO made it, to me the open areas of this game felt so much more fun to play than the linear sequences that I just couldn't help making the comparison.

I'm no James Bond expert, having seen only a few of the movies.  However, to me, 007 First Light really nails the James Bond tone perfectly.  The semi-futuristic gadgets and suave characters make you feel very much like you're playing one of the movies.  The conspiracy story, and traveling through environments such as a black-tie museum party and a remote Antarctic base, sell the theme just as much as the iconic music does.

The game is structured into two types of levels.  There are more open levels that evoke a Hitman-lite structure.  You might need to find a suspicious individual in a hotel, or navigate a nightclub to try to gather some intel.  While these levels didn't give you Hitman's full freedom of improvisation, I still found these levels to be the most fun in the game.

Other levels are more linear action set-pieces and combat encounters.  While these could be fun, I did bristle a bit at the loss of choice you have during them.  If the game wants you to be having a gunfight, you will be having a gunfight and there won't be an alternate way to stealth or puzzle your way through the situation.  Since I find it a lot more fun to think of different ways to approach encounters, this always felt a bit disappointing to me.

That being said, the combat is quite fun.  Guns felt a bit overpowered in Bond's hands, but that's counteracted by the fact that in a typical gunfight you're heavily outnumbered and using extremely limited ammunition.  Fist fighting feels even better, using a typical system of dodging or parrying based upon what color the enemy flashes and hitting them back with combos once you've successfully dodged.  A real highlight of the combat is the environmental interaction.  In nearly all the combat arenas you'll find tons of interactions available to spice up the encounters, from railings to throw enemies over to electric panels to toss them into.

Stealth in the game is also set up in a very fun way.  If you break stealth, it's really only the enemies in the immediate vicinity who will converge on you.  This means that if you happen to get caught, you're not just going full combat for the rest of the mission, which is really nice.  Of course, if you take a step back and realize that the enemies in the next room don't seem to care that there's an absolute brawl going on next door, it makes less sense.  Still, I appreciate the choice to optimize for fun.

Stealth is also helped by the ability for Bond to smooth-talk his way out of being caught in most situations.  Doing this costs you Instinct Points, which are earned by doing other combat actions, including performing stealth takedowns, which means you can't abuse it but rather use it as an occasional tool in your stealth toolbox.

Unfortunately for me, while the beginning of the game tends to alternate between the open areas and the action levels, as the game moves into its third act it leans much more heavily toward the action.  While this was necessary to drive the plot forward to its conclusion, it meant that the back half of the game was mostly made up of the levels I liked the least.

007 First Light also does a great job creating checkpoints that are just granular enough to make going back to specific areas of the game very easy. That, in turn, made mopping up the collectibles something that was fun to do rather than a slog.

In addition to the main story, there is an optional set of combat challenges in the TacSim that you can play.  These didn't really grab me, and I bounced off them fairly quickly.  They are completely optional though, and I didn't feel at all like I was going to be forced to play through them for in-game benefits.

I had the most fun in 007 First Light when it invited me to solve open-world puzzles using my own guile, and found it frustrating when it simply forced me to have a gunfight.  While I had a good time playing it, I do wish it were a bit more of a 007 Hitman game than an action game made by the Hitman people.
