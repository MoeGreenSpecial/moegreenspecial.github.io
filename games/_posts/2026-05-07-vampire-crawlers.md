Vampire Survivors is a hall of fame game for me, and deck building games are one of my favorite genres, so Vampire Crawlers feels like it may have been built with me in mind.  And wow, does it deliver on that promise.  

At its core, this is really a deck building game, with a Vampire Survivors theme.  The gameplay mechanics are interesting - you're attempting to build a deck where you can play cards sequentially from low cost to high cost to increase your combo as you go. The combo, in turn, makes each card more effective when you play it.

The early game is about picking cards that have enough spread over different costs to enable you to build combos, while also creating enough of a balance between cards that add mana, cards that increase your character's abilities, attack, and defense cards.  Eventually, you'll find wildcards that let you play any cost card next and continue the combo.  By the mid-to-late game, with enough wildcards and cards that let you draw, you can wind up making effectively infinite combos and breaking the game wide open.  Getting that engine setup is extremely satisfying, and wiping through the end part of dungeons as an unstoppable wielder of infinite cards is really fun.

You can power up cards by slotting different effect gems into slots on them, and this becomes another fun part of the game strategy - deciding which cards the gems would have the best use on.

These gems also provide meta-progression in the game; between runs you can spend money on adding additional gem slots to the cards and also increase or decrease the rarity of the individual gems.  Vampire Crawlers also retains the meta-progression of Vampire Survivors where you can spend money to permanently increase your stats.  Curiously, you can always refund the money spent on stat upgrades if you want to redo them, however that same option is not available for the gem upgrades.

The Vampire Survivors portion of this game is mostly thematic, and Vampire Crawlers really nails that.  The levels, enemies, and all the weapons and power ups on the cards are taken directly from Vampire Survivors.  If you know the combinations of things that work well together in Vampire Survivors, those same combinations will work in Vampire Crawlers.  The work that was done to integrate the theme makes this truly play as a Vampire Survivors card game, and is incredibly well done.

One of the mechanics that carries over from Vampire Survivors is weapon evolutions, where if you have a specific combination of weapons and other items in your deck, you can evolve the weapon into a more powerful version.  In Vampire Survivors this is a core mechanic, and the most effective runs focus on making sure that you're pairing up your weapons with their corresponding evolution requirements.  Since the chests which allow for evolving weapons in that game drop fairly frequently, it's not uncommon to finish a game with nearly all of your weapons evolved if you play it right.

This is something I missed from Vampire Crawlers, where Evolution Gems are significantly rarer.  Frequently, I would finish a run having found only one or two of the gems needed to trigger an evolution, which could be frustrating if you were holding onto a card only because you wanted to use it for the evolution.  Several of the cards that are needed to evolve weapons (such as Stone Mask and Tirajisu) are single-use cards that are destroyed when you use them.  Having to hold them in deck and be very careful not to use them, hoping for an Evolution Gem to pop up just doesn't feel great.

While the characters in Vampire Survivors do all have differences between them, by the time you've played that game to completion I found that they aren't very pronounced.  In contrast, the character choices in Vampire Crawlers play extremely differently, with different bonuses when their cards are played and while they are active.  The bonuses greatly change how you approach building your deck and approaching a run, and that adds a nice variety to the game.  Additionally, through upgrades you can bring up to three different crawlers with you on a run (and sometimes find additional ones while you are in a dungeon), which leads to the ability to build some crawler combinations that really synergize quite well.  However, much like how I wound up defaulting to Queen Sigma in Vampire Survivors, I kept coming back to the combination of Pugnala, Gallo, and Giovanna.

The difficulty ramp in Vampire Crawlers also contrasts with the ramp in Vampire Survivors.  In Vampire Survivors, it can take several runs to get past the first few minutes of the first level, whereas Vampire Crawlers felt a bit more approachable out of the gate.  There were really only two spots in the game where I felt like I wasn't yet strong enough to proceed and needed to grind for some money to upgrade my stats.  That meant that there wasn't the satisfying meta-game feeling of finally overcoming what had previously been impossible, however I felt like that was replaced by the dopamine hit of building a deck that really popped off in a dungeon.

I did find myself wishing for some quality of life upgrades while I was playing.  When picking new abilities, Vampire Survivors shows you if any of your choices synergize with any of your existing items.  While Vampire Crawlers does have a Grim Grimoire available from the pause menu, it's not available when selecting a card nor is there any indication of a synergy that might exist.  It's not hard to look the information up, but having it easily available in game would be better.

Another quality of life upgrade I wish existed is the ability to sort your hand.  Toward the end of a run, if you've managed to put together a build that can run huge combos with lots of draws, you wind up with so many cards in your hand it's very difficult to see what's available.  Since you're effectively always looking for a card with a specific cost or a wild card, having the cards be sorted by cost would be an incredible feature to have.  It's a small thing, but it would have gone a long way toward smoothing out the end game.

In all, Vampire Crawlers was an incredibly fun experience. I don't think it quite rises to the hall of fame level of Vampire Survivors, however I had a blast playing it and I really hope it gets as much of a post-launch update cycle as Vampire Survivors did.  This is one I'd love to revisit again once or twice a year to run through new content drops and play again and again.
