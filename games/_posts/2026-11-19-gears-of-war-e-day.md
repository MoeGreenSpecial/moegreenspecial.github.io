Gears of War: E-Day is more Gears of War, and one of the best entries in the series.  However, that alone doesn't elevate it into elite company.  E-Day knows what it wants to do, and does it well, but doesn't try to reach beyond being a Gears of War game.

As a Gears game, you'll be doing a lot of cover shooting. You can't play this like Halo or Call of Duty, and I like that Gears has its own identity and play style among the shooters.  Gunfights are always fun and intense. There's a great variety of unique weapons, each with their own use, but I did find myself using my favorite loadout for most of the game.  I also liked the concept of finding collectible mods for your weapons, but in practice the weapon variety and the fact that I stuck with just a few weapons meant that most of the mods I found I never used.

Playing single-player, I found myself gravitating to Reyes' Signature Longshot as an extremely effective weapon during fights.  However, I fell into a comfort zone of sticking back sniping enemies while the rest of my team handled the front line.

There are also a few fights which are structured as mini-Horde modes, which were fun, but I felt they didn't go quite far enough.  There weren't enough waves that made it a strict necessity to set up defenses.  And when I did spend the time to set up a lot of defenses, it felt as though they would just shred through the enemies without much effort.

E-Day is a fairly forgiving game, especially with the Down-but-not-Out system, although there were certainly big difficulty spikes during boss encounters.  One fight near the end in particular sees you fighting two giant Locust who can fly, have fairly small weak points, and can both do large area-of-effect attacks.  It felt as though that fight could have been better balanced, perhaps with only one enemy or with some more forgiving terrain to battle in.  Gears of War: E-Day never really forced me to change how I played, and the difficulty spikes felt more like balance issues than an attempt at making me adapt.

The Coalition are experts in the Unreal Engine, and it shows.  The graphics in Gears of War: E-Day are among the best in recent memory.  The lighting in particular stands out as a highlight.

The fairly lengthy campaign has an interesting structure that fits well within the story being told of the day that the Locust emerged from below Sera.  At the beginning of the game, while none of the characters knows who the Locust are, it feels a bit like a horror game.  As the characters come to terms with what they're dealing with, the game becomes more and more of the third-person cover shooter that the Gears series is known for being.

There are several chapters that feature an open map instead of the more typical linear map structure.  E-Day handles this much better than Gears 5 did.  Whereas in Gears 5, I felt like the open maps were just large spaces to traverse in order to get to the next mission, in E-Day they are based in cities and are much more densely packed.  You'll naturally come across side-missions with people needing help, which feels like a great use of the space.  Even better, since many of the open-map areas revisit the city of Kalona as the Locust invasion is proceeding, you get to see the city crumbling from the inside over time.  It's a very effective use of the idea.

I'm not deep into Gears lore, although I have played all the games and know the big story beats.  That made the story of E-Day play well for me, but I do wish they would have spent more time on Marcus and Dom's relationship.  The two of them form the core of the story of the first three games, and as a prequel I thought that they should have spent more time setting that up.  The new characters weren't very well-developed, and I think that while both Lucas and Mags had hints of interesting back-story the game didn't do enough to progress their characters to make them stand out for me.

The introduction of the chainsaw and lancer was an incredible story beat.  After your team discovers how effective a chainsaw is against the Locust, and you share how much better it is than a bayonet with the quartermaster back at camp, watching the lightbulb go off over his head had me grinning from ear to ear.

I get it, Gears of War has a somewhat limited audience these days.  And The Coalition ending the game by having Marcus Fenix looking directly into the camera while Metallica plays in the background says to me that they know exactly who that audience is.  Count me among them.
