Drop Duchy manages to be a Tetris-like roguelite deck building city-builder, while at the same time not really being any of those things at all.

On the surface, it appears to be a Tetris-like game.  But, as I quickly found out, playing it as if it were Tetris is a quick path to failure.  You need to be much more focused on strategically placing tiles than you do on ensuring that you don't leave any open spaces.  Tetris rewards you for a clean board, Drop Duchy rewards you for strategic tile placement.  Learning to be OK with some gaps if it meant that I was able to place a Tetrimino in a more advantageous place was the first step I had to take toward being able to win a run of Drop Duchy.

It's a deck-builder, because you're selecting cards that will eventually form the basis of your Tetris pieces.  But, you're also only bringing a small handful of cards with you into each battle, so it's not really a full deck builder either.

Many of those cards contain city pieces, making this a city-builder.  Except that you're also focused on gathering resources and stocking up a military in each puzzle, so it's not really a city builder either.

It's a strategy game, which contains several factions, each of which plays quite differently.  The Duchy is focused on switching terrain types and resource production, while The Republic is trying to build the largest city, and The Order uses faith to boost resource production, and crusaders to generate upgrades.  This variety makes each run feel different and not just like you're repeating a solved puzzle every time.

Drop Duchy isn't any one genre, or a combination of them, it's just Drop Duchy.  And that's OK by me, because it turns out Drop Duchy is a really fun and unique way to spend an hour of puzzle time.
